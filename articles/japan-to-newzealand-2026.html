Working Holiday Visa

New Zealand has a bilateral Working Holiday agreement with Japan offering one of the most accessible routes for young Japanese workers aged 18–30. The fee is NZD 770 and the visa grants a 12-month open work permit — allowing work for any employer. Unlike Australia's WHV, there is no regional-work extension in New Zealand; the Working Holiday is generally a single 12-month visa (a Working Holiday Extension Work Visa may be possible in some industries), and a second Working Holiday is not available for Japanese nationals. However, the experience gained, along with English skill development, makes the NZ Working Holiday an excellent first step toward longer-term migration.

New Zealand's adult minimum wage is NZD 23.95 per hour (from 1 April 2026), and popular job sectors for WHV holders include hospitality, agriculture, tourism, and retail. New Zealand's stunning natural environment — fjords, mountains, geothermal parks — makes it particularly popular with Japanese outdoor enthusiasts. The country's smaller size (5 million population) means the job market is more limited than Australia, but cost of living outside Auckland is manageable.

Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV)

For skilled professionals not eligible for the Working Holiday, the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) is the main employer-sponsored work visa. Introduced in 2022, it requires the employer to be accredited with Immigration New Zealand, to advertise the role locally first (unless exempt), and to offer a job paying at least the market rate (the general median-wage requirement was removed in March 2025). You need at least 2 years of relevant experience or a Level 4+ qualification. The AEWV is employer-tied and lasts up to 5 years. Construction, healthcare, IT, and engineering are among the sectors with active employer demand for skilled foreign workers including Japanese professionals.

Specific Purpose Work Visas

  • Religious Worker Visa: For those employed by a religious organisation

Student Visa

Japanese students studying in New Zealand for longer than 3 months must obtain a Student Visa (fee: NZD 850). Applications are submitted online through Immigration New Zealand. Requirements include an offer of place from a New Zealand Tertiary Education Commission (TEC) registered institution, proof of financial means (NZD 20,000/year for living costs plus tuition), health insurance, and a genuine intention to study. IELTS scores of 5.5–6.5 are typically required depending on the level of study. New Zealand universities are well-regarded internationally and tuition is lower than Australia or the UK — ranging from NZD 22,000 to NZD 32,000 per year for most programs.

Post-Study Work Rights

New Zealand offers generous post-study work rights for international graduates. Eligible graduates qualify for an open Post-Study Work Visa of up to 3 years: master's and doctoral graduates (with 30+ weeks of study in New Zealand) get 3 years, and bachelor's and other eligible qualifications get a visa matching the length of study. From 16 November 2026 a new 6-month Short-Term Graduate Work Visa is planned for eligible Level 5–7 graduates. This period provides New Zealand work experience that counts toward the Skilled Migrant Category. Student visa holders can work up to 25 hours per week during term time (raised from 20 in November 2025) and full time during holidays.

Skilled Migrant Category (SMC)

The Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) is New Zealand's main skilled residence pathway. Since October 2023 it uses a simplified 6-point system: points for a recognised qualification (bachelor's 3, master's 5, doctorate 6), New Zealand occupational registration, or high income, plus up to 3 points for skilled work in New Zealand. Age and regional bonus points no longer apply, and you need a skilled job or job offer in New Zealand. From 24 August 2026, new Skilled Work Experience and Trades & Technician pathways also opened. Japanese applicants with New Zealand work experience from an AEWV are well-positioned to reach 6 points.

Green List (Straight to Residence)

New Zealand's Green List identifies occupations in critical shortage where qualified workers can apply directly for residence without going through the SMC points pool. Tier 1 of the Green List allows direct residence applications (Straight to Residence) for roles such as doctors, registered nurses, midwives, secondary teachers, and certain engineers. Tier 2 allows a work-to-residence pathway after 2 years in the role. Japanese professionals in healthcare and engineering who meet the qualification and registration requirements should check whether their occupation is on the Green List — this is the fastest route to NZ residence.

Investor and Entrepreneur Visas

  • Active Investor Plus: NZD 5 million (Growth category) or NZD 10 million (Balanced category) invested in New Zealand over 3 years; no English requirement since April 2025; leads to residence
  • Entrepreneur Resident Visa: Business routes exist for people who establish or buy a business in New Zealand — settings changed in 2025, so check Immigration NZ for the current options
  • Citizenship is available after 5 years of permanent residence — New Zealand permits dual nationality, which is advantageous for Japanese applicants

NZeTA and International Visitor Levy

Japanese passport holders can visit New Zealand visa-free for up to 3 months but must obtain a New Zealand Electronic Travel Authority (NZeTA) before departure. The NZeTA costs NZD 17 through the official app or NZD 23 on the website. In addition, most visitors pay the International Visitor Levy (IVL) of NZD 100 (raised from NZD 35 in October 2024), which funds New Zealand's tourism infrastructure and conservation. The NZeTA and IVL are paid together during the online application and the NZeTA is valid for multiple visits over 2 years. Processing is typically instant but can take up to 72 hours.

Flight Routes

  • Tokyo (NRT) to Auckland (AKL): approximately 11 hours direct (Air New Zealand, ANA)
  • Some routes connect via Sydney or Singapore adding 3–4 hours transit time
  • Christchurch (CHC) and Wellington (WLG) are accessible from Auckland by domestic Air New Zealand flights

Travel Highlights for Japanese Visitors

New Zealand is a dream destination for Japanese nature lovers. The country is renowned for its dramatic, unspoilt landscapes — Milford Sound, Queenstown, Rotorua's geothermal parks, the Bay of Islands, and the Southern Alps are world-class attractions. The Lord of the Rings and Hobbit film locations draw significant Japanese fan tourism. New Zealand drives on the left — comfortable for Japanese visitors. Self-drive road trips in a campervan are extremely popular with Japanese travelers. The country has strict biosecurity laws similar to Australia: all food, plant material, and animal products must be declared on arrival. Fines for non-declaration are steep.
