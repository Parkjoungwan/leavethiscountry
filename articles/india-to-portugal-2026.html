Job Seeker Visa

Portugal offers a Skilled Job Seeker Visa allowing Indian professionals with specialised technical skills to enter Portugal for 120 days to find employment. Requirements include proof of qualifications (recognized degree), proof of financial means (benchmarked to Portugal's €920/month minimum wage in 2026), and health insurance. Since the October 2025 foreigners-law reform (Law 61/2025) this visa is limited to qualified profiles and must be followed by an AIMA appointment; once a job is secured, you convert to a residence permit for employed persons. This is an excellent route for Indian IT professionals targeting Lisbon and Porto's growing tech scenes — home to Web Summit and numerous fintech and software companies including Farfetch, OutSystems, and Feedzai.

Highly Qualified Activity Visa & EU Blue Card

Portugal's highly qualified activity residence visa (often called D3) and the EU Blue Card are the fast-track routes for degree-level professionals with a concrete job offer:

  • Requires a highly qualified job offer or contract (Blue Card offers must also meet the salary threshold set by AIMA)
  • Or a role with a company certified under Portugal's Tech Visa programme
  • 2-year renewable residence permit, pathway to 5-year PR
  • Family reunification possible — check current AIMA rules, as Law 61/2025 introduced a 2-year residence requirement for many permit types
  • No language requirement at entry (Portuguese integration support provided)

Digital Nomad Visa (D8)

The D8 Digital Nomad Visa is highly popular with Indian remote workers. Requirements: proof of remote income of at least €3,680/month (4× Portugal's €920 minimum wage, 2026) from a non-Portuguese employer or clients. The residence permit issued by AIMA is valid for 2 years and renewable for 3, leading to permanent residence after 5 years. Lisbon and Porto offer co-working spaces, affordable compared to Western Europe, and a vibrant expat community. The old NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) regime closed to new applicants in 2024; its replacement, IFICI (often called NHR 2.0), offers a 20% flat rate on eligible income for 10 years but only for specific qualifying roles (research, highly qualified jobs in eligible companies, certified startups).

University Landscape

Portugal's public universities are affordable with tuition ranging from €950–6,000/year. Top institutions include the University of Lisbon (ULisboa), University of Porto, NOVA University Lisbon, and Técnico Lisboa (engineering). For Indian students, the key challenge is language — most undergraduate programs are in Portuguese. However, English-taught Master's programs are expanding rapidly, particularly in computer science, data science, finance, and engineering at Técnico Lisboa and NOVA SBE (business school). IST (Instituto Superior Técnico) ranks among Europe's top engineering schools.

Student Visa (D4) Requirements

  • Acceptance letter from a Portuguese institution
  • Proof of financial means benchmarked to Portugal's minimum wage (€920/month in 2026)
  • Health insurance or proof of access to health services
  • Accommodation proof in Portugal
  • Criminal background check from India (apostilled)
  • Apply via the Portuguese Embassy in New Delhi, the Consulate General in Goa, or VFS Global Portugal centres
  • Processing: 60–90 days; apply early

Post-Study and Scholarships

After completing studies in Portugal, graduates can apply for a job search residence permit valid 1 year to find employment. If hired, convert to a work residence permit. Living costs in Lisbon are higher than elsewhere in Portugal (€1,000–1,500/month including rent); Porto and Coimbra are more affordable at €700–1,000/month. The Portuguese government and Camões Institute offer limited scholarships for Indian students; university-specific merit scholarships and Erasmus+ funding are more commonly available for students already in the EU/EEA but Portugal-based programs may provide institutional grants.

D7 Passive Income Visa

The D7 Visa is designed for individuals with regular passive income from pensions, rental income, dividends, or other sources. Minimum income: €920/month (Portuguese minimum wage, 2026). The main applicant needs €920/month + 50% (€460) per additional adult + 30% (€276) per child. After approval (apply at Portuguese consulate in India), register with AIMA (Agency for Integration, Migration and Asylum) in Portugal within 4 months for your residence permit. The AIMA registration process has faced significant delays (6–18 months backlog in 2024–2026) due to high demand from international applicants.

Golden Visa (ARI)

Portugal's Golden Visa (ARI) program was modified in 2023 — real estate investment (including the former low-density-area option) no longer qualifies anywhere in Portugal, and the €1.5M capital-transfer route was abolished. Current qualifying investments:

  • Fund investment: €500,000 minimum in approved Portuguese investment funds
  • Cultural or heritage donation: €250,000
  • Job creation: 10 permanent jobs in Portugal
  • Research contribution: €500,000 to research activities
  • Residence permit valid 2 years, then renewable for 3-year periods; PR eligible after 5 years; citizenship after 10 years under the 2026 nationality law
  • Only requires 7 days/year physical presence in Portugal

Permanent Residence and Citizenship

After 5 years of legal residence, Indian nationals can apply for permanent residence (Autorização de Residência Permanente). Portuguese citizenship by naturalization now requires 10 years of legal residence (Lei Orgânica 1/2026, in force since 19 May 2026; counted from the first residence permit), A2 Portuguese, knowledge of Portuguese culture and civic principles, and a clean criminal record. Portugal permits dual citizenship, but India does not — Indian nationals who naturalise must give up Indian citizenship (OCI status is available). Portuguese citizenship grants EU citizenship, providing free movement, work, and residence rights across all 27 EU member states — a major draw for Indian professionals thinking long-term.

Schengen Visa via Portugal

Indian citizens need a Schengen short-stay visa (Type C) to visit Portugal. If Portugal is your primary destination or longest stay, apply at the Portuguese Consulate in Mumbai or New Delhi, or through VFS Global Portugal. Fee: €90 (plus VFS service charge). Processing: 15 calendar days standard (up to 45 days maximum). The Schengen visa allows access to all 29 Schengen countries. On arrival, the EU Entry/Exit System (EES, fully live since April 2026) records your fingerprints and photo instead of a passport stamp. Portugal is increasingly popular as a European travel destination for Indians — offering lower costs than Paris or Amsterdam with equally stunning culture, history, and food.

Required Documents

  • Valid passport (3+ months beyond return date)
  • Completed Schengen application form + 2 photos
  • Travel health insurance (€30,000 minimum, valid across Schengen)
  • Flight bookings (return tickets)
  • Hotel reservations or accommodation proof
  • Bank statements showing sufficient funds (€75–100/day minimum)
  • Leave letter from employer + recent salary slips
  • Detailed travel itinerary

Portugal Travel Highlights

Portugal offers Lisbon and Porto as world-class city destinations, plus the Algarve (beaches), Sintra (palaces), Douro Valley (wine country), and Madeira (year-round warm weather). The Goa-Portugal historical connection means many Goan Indians have Portuguese ancestry and may even qualify for Portuguese citizenship by descent — check eligibility through the Portuguese Consulate in Goa. Flights from India to Lisbon typically connect through London, Amsterdam, Paris, Dubai, or Doha — no direct flights currently. Travel time is approximately 12–16 hours with a layover.
